Everyday Life & Practical Knowledge
=====================================

TIME & CALENDARS
-----------------
The world uses the Gregorian calendar (365 days/year, 366 in leap years).
A leap year occurs every 4 years (divisible by 4), except centuries not divisible by 400.
Example: 2000 was a leap year; 1900 was not.

Time zones:
  UTC (Coordinated Universal Time): The world's time standard
  UTC+3: Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait (Arabia Standard Time)
  UTC+0: UK (winter), Ghana, Iceland
  UTC-5: Eastern USA (winter)
  UTC+8: China, Singapore
  UTC+9: Japan, South Korea

Days of the week: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday
Months: January(31), February(28/29), March(31), April(30), May(31), June(30),
         July(31), August(31), September(30), October(31), November(30), December(31)

MEASUREMENTS & UNITS
---------------------
Length:
  1 meter = 100 centimeters = 1000 millimeters
  1 kilometer = 1000 meters
  1 foot = 30.48 cm; 1 inch = 2.54 cm; 1 mile = 1.609 km

Weight/Mass:
  1 kilogram = 1000 grams
  1 pound = 0.453 kg; 1 ounce = 28.35 grams
  1 ton (metric) = 1000 kg

Temperature conversions:
  °F to °C: (°F - 32) × 5/9
  °C to °F: (°C × 9/5) + 32
  Common values: 0°C = 32°F (freezing), 100°C = 212°F (boiling), 37°C = 98.6°F (body)

Volume:
  1 liter = 1000 milliliters
  1 US gallon = 3.785 liters; 1 US cup = 237 ml

COOKING & KITCHEN
------------------
Common cooking methods:
  Boiling: Cooking in water at 100°C
  Baking/Roasting: Dry heat in an oven (150-250°C)
  Frying: Cooking in hot oil (175-190°C)
  Steaming: Using steam from boiling water (healthy, preserves nutrients)
  Grilling: Direct heat from below or above

Common kitchen temperatures:
  Refrigerator: 2-4°C
  Freezer: -18°C
  Boiling water: 100°C
  Bread baking: 180-200°C
  Steak well-done: internal 70°C

Food safety:
  Danger zone: 4°C to 60°C (bacteria multiply rapidly)
  Always thaw food in the refrigerator, not at room temperature
  Wash hands before and after handling raw meat
  Cook chicken to internal temperature of at least 74°C

MONEY & ECONOMICS
------------------
Common world currencies:
  USD ($): US Dollar — world's primary reserve currency
  EUR (€): Euro — used by 20 EU countries
  GBP (£): British Pound Sterling
  JPY (¥): Japanese Yen
  IQD: Iraqi Dinar
  SAR: Saudi Riyal
  AED: UAE Dirham

Basic economic concepts:
  Inflation: Rise in prices over time; reduces purchasing power
  GDP: Gross Domestic Product — total value of goods/services produced in a country
  Interest rate: Cost of borrowing money; set by central banks
  Stock market: Where company shares are bought and sold

TRANSPORTATION
--------------
Speed comparisons:
  Walking: ~5 km/h
  Cycling: ~15-25 km/h
  Car (city): 30-60 km/h
  Car (highway): 80-120 km/h
  Train (fast): 200-350 km/h
  Commercial aircraft: ~900 km/h
  Sound: 343 m/s = 1235 km/h
  Light: 299,792 km/s

Fuel economy:
  Liters per 100 km (L/100km) — lower is more efficient
  Miles per gallon (MPG) — higher is more efficient
  Conversion: 100 ÷ L/100km × 0.4251 = MPG

WEATHER & CLIMATE
-----------------
Weather terms:
  Temperature: How hot or cold the air is
  Humidity: Amount of water vapor in the air (0-100%)
  Precipitation: Water falling from clouds (rain, snow, sleet, hail)
  Wind speed: Measured in km/h or m/s; Beaufort scale 0-12
  Barometric pressure: Air pressure; falling = incoming bad weather

Weather phenomena:
  Thunderstorm: Heavy rain + lightning + thunder (caused by convection)
  Hurricane/Typhoon/Cyclone: Same thing — large rotating storm over warm ocean
  Tornado: Violent rotating column of air touching the ground
  Fog: Low-lying cloud reducing visibility; forms when air cools to dew point

SPACE & THE SOLAR SYSTEM
--------------------------
The Sun: A star about 1.4 million km in diameter; 150 million km from Earth
Planets (in order from the Sun):
  1. Mercury — closest to Sun; no atmosphere; extreme temperatures
  2. Venus — hottest planet (462°C); covered in thick CO₂ clouds
  3. Earth — only known planet with life; 71% water surface
  4. Mars — called the Red Planet; has the largest volcano (Olympus Mons)
  5. Jupiter — largest planet; Great Red Spot is a 400-year-old storm
  6. Saturn — known for its stunning ring system; lower density than water
  7. Uranus — rotates on its side; made of ice giants
  8. Neptune — farthest planet; fastest winds in the solar system (2100 km/h)

Moon: Earth's only natural satellite; 384,400 km from Earth
A light-year: Distance light travels in one year ≈ 9.46 trillion km
Nearest star to Earth: Proxima Centauri (4.24 light-years away)
Milky Way galaxy: Contains 100-400 billion stars; our Sun is one of them
Observable universe: Estimated to contain 2 trillion galaxies
