# World & General Knowledge Reference

## Geography

### Continents of the World
1. **Asia** — Largest continent; includes China, India, Japan, Iraq, Saudi Arabia
2. **Africa** — Second largest; 54 countries; Nile River is the world's longest river
3. **North America** — Includes USA, Canada, Mexico; Grand Canyon, Rocky Mountains
4. **South America** — Includes Brazil, Argentina; Amazon River, Andes Mountains
5. **Europe** — Includes UK, France, Germany, Italy, Spain; 44 countries
6. **Australia/Oceania** — Smallest continent; includes Australia, New Zealand
7. **Antarctica** — Southernmost continent; covered in ice; no permanent residents

### Major Countries & Capitals
- Iraq → Baghdad (Tigris & Euphrates rivers civilization)
- Saudi Arabia → Riyadh
- USA → Washington D.C.
- UK → London
- France → Paris
- Germany → Berlin
- China → Beijing
- Japan → Tokyo
- India → New Delhi
- Brazil → Brasília
- Russia → Moscow

### Notable Geographic Facts
- Earth's circumference: ~40,075 km
- Highest mountain: Mount Everest (8,849 m)
- Deepest ocean point: Mariana Trench (~11,034 m)
- Largest ocean: Pacific Ocean
- Largest desert: Sahara (hot), Antarctica (cold)
- Longest river: Nile River (~6,650 km)

## History

### Ancient Civilizations
- **Mesopotamia** (Modern Iraq): First writing (cuneiform), first cities, Code of Hammurabi
- **Ancient Egypt**: Pyramids, hieroglyphics, Nile-based agriculture
- **Ancient Greece**: Democracy, philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), Olympics
- **Roman Empire**: Law systems, Latin language base, engineering (roads, aqueducts)
- **Islamic Golden Age** (750–1258 CE): Algebra, astronomy, medicine, House of Wisdom in Baghdad

### Key Events in Modern History
- 1776: American Declaration of Independence
- 1789: French Revolution
- 1905: Einstein's Theory of Relativity
- 1945: End of World War II; United Nations founded
- 1969: First humans on the Moon (Apollo 11)
- 1991: World Wide Web created by Tim Berners-Lee
- 2007: First iPhone released
- 2022: ChatGPT launched, marking mass adoption of AI assistants

## Science

### Physics
- **Newton's Laws**: Objects at rest stay at rest; F = m×a; equal and opposite reactions
- **Speed of Light**: 299,792,458 m/s in vacuum
- **Gravity**: 9.8 m/s² on Earth's surface
- **Energy**: E = mc² (Einstein); energy and matter are interchangeable
- **Thermodynamics**: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted

### Chemistry
- **Atoms**: Smallest unit of an element; made of protons, neutrons, electrons
- **Elements**: 118 known elements; hydrogen is the lightest and most abundant
- **Water (H₂O)**: Two hydrogen atoms + one oxygen atom
- **pH Scale**: 0-14; below 7 = acidic, 7 = neutral, above 7 = basic
- **Carbon**: The basis of all life; forms millions of organic compounds

### Biology
- **DNA**: The genetic code of life; double helix structure; discovered 1953
- **Cell**: Basic unit of life; humans have ~37 trillion cells
- **Evolution**: Species change over generations through natural selection (Darwin)
- **Photosynthesis**: Plants convert sunlight + CO₂ + water → glucose + oxygen
- **Human Body**: 206 bones, ~600 muscles, ~100 billion neurons in the brain

## Mathematics

### Key Concepts
- **Pi (π)**: 3.14159... — ratio of circle circumference to diameter
- **Pythagorean Theorem**: a² + b² = c² for right triangles
- **Prime Numbers**: Numbers divisible only by 1 and themselves (2, 3, 5, 7, 11...)
- **Fibonacci Sequence**: 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21... (each = sum of previous two)
- **Logarithm**: Inverse of exponentiation; log₁₀(1000) = 3

### Number Systems
- Binary (Base 2): 0, 1 — used in computers
- Octal (Base 8): 0-7 — used in some programming
- Decimal (Base 10): 0-9 — everyday use
- Hexadecimal (Base 16): 0-9, A-F — used in colors (#FF5733) and memory addresses

## Health & Human Body

### Basic Nutrition
- **Carbohydrates**: Primary energy source; found in bread, rice, fruits
- **Proteins**: Build and repair tissue; found in meat, eggs, beans, nuts
- **Fats**: Energy storage and cell function; healthy fats in olive oil, avocado, fish
- **Vitamins**: Organic compounds essential in small amounts (A, B, C, D, E, K)
- **Minerals**: Inorganic elements needed for body functions (calcium, iron, zinc)
- **Water**: ~60% of the human body; essential for all biological processes

### Vital Signs (Normal Range for Adults)
- Body temperature: 36.5–37.5°C (97.7–99.5°F)
- Heart rate: 60–100 beats per minute
- Blood pressure: ~120/80 mmHg
- Respiratory rate: 12–20 breaths per minute
- Blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂): 95–100%

### Common Ailments
- **Fever**: Temperature above 38°C (100.4°F); sign of infection
- **Hypertension**: High blood pressure; silent disease; managed with diet and medication
- **Diabetes**: Insufficient insulin production or resistance; affects blood sugar
- **Asthma**: Chronic lung condition causing breathing difficulty; triggered by allergens

## Environment & Energy

### Renewable Energy Sources
- **Solar Power**: Converts sunlight to electricity via photovoltaic panels
- **Wind Power**: Wind turbines generate electricity from moving air
- **Hydropower**: Water flow drives turbines (dams)
- **Geothermal**: Heat from Earth's core converted to electricity

### Climate Change
- Greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, water vapor) trap heat in the atmosphere
- Global average temperature has risen ~1.1°C since pre-industrial times
- Effects: More extreme weather, sea level rise, ecosystem disruption
- Solutions: Renewable energy, energy efficiency, reforestation, carbon capture
